EXPERIMENT 01
PWM-BASED VACUUM RESPONSE CHARACTERIZATION

Date:
14 July 2026

Project:
Development of an Embedded Pressure-Control System for a Biomimetic Breast Pump

Objective:
To characterize the vacuum pressure response of the diaphragm pump
at 80% and 100% PWM duty cycles using BMP280-based pressure
measurement.

Experimental Setup:
- Microcontroller: Raspberry Pi Pico 2W
- Firmware: MicroPython
- Motor driver: L298N
- Pump: KMDP diaphragm pump
- Pressure sensor: BMP280
- Communication: I2C
- Pressure measurement: Atmospheric baseline referenced vacuum
- Pneumatic configuration: Pump connected to test chamber
- BMP280 placed within temporary sealed pneumatic chamber

Test Parameters:
PWM levels: 80%, 100%
Settling time: 3 seconds
Measurement time: 20 seconds per PWM level
Sampling delay: 0.1 seconds
Pressure unit: mmHg

Atmospheric Baseline:
706.04 mmHg

RESULTS:

80% PWM
Average vacuum: -2.83 mmHg
Maximum measured vacuum: -2.98 mmHg

100% PWM
Average vacuum: -4.88 mmHg
Maximum measured vacuum: -6.45 mmHg

OBSERVATIONS:
1. A measurable reduction in chamber pressure was observed with
   increasing PWM duty cycle.

2. At 80% PWM, the pressure response gradually approached an
   approximately steady vacuum region.

3. Increasing the PWM duty cycle to 100% resulted in a larger
   pressure differential.

4. The 100% PWM test was performed immediately after the 80% PWM
   test without venting the chamber. Therefore, the 100% test
   represents a progressive pressure response rather than an
   independent atmospheric-baseline pump-down test.

5. No visually observed motor stalling occurred during this test.

CONCLUSION:
The experiment demonstrated that PWM-controlled pump actuation
produced a measurable vacuum response that could be detected using
the BMP280 pressure sensor. The results supported further evaluation
of the pump-pressure system for feedback control.
