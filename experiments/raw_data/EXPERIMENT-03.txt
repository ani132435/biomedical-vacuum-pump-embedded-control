EXPERIMENT-03

Date: 14 July 2026
Controller: Raspberry Pi Pico 2W
Firmware: MicroPython
Motor Driver: L298N
Pressure Sensor: BMP280
Pressure Interface: I2C
PWM Frequency: 1000 Hz

OBJECTIVE:
To characterize the vacuum pressure response of the diaphragm pump
using a simplified direct pneumatic connection between the pump and
the airtight BMP280 test chamber.

PNEUMATIC CONFIGURATION:
Diaphragm Pump -> Airtight BMP280 Test Chamber

The previously used T-junction and moving diaphragm branch were
removed for this test.

REASON FOR CONFIGURATION CHANGE:
The branched pneumatic configuration introduced additional tubing,
pneumatic volume, a compliant moving diaphragm, and possible leakage
paths. A direct pump-to-test-chamber connection was therefore used
to isolate and characterize the pump pressure response.

TEST CONDITIONS:
PWM levels: 80%, 100%
Settling time: 3 s
Measurement time: 20 s per PWM level
Sampling interval: approximately 0.1 s
Atmospheric calibration samples: 30

CALIBRATED ATMOSPHERIC PRESSURE:
705.02 mmHg

FINAL RESULTS:

80% PWM:
Average vacuum = -2.83 mmHg
Maximum measured vacuum = -2.98 mmHg

100% PWM:
Average vacuum = -4.88 mmHg
Maximum measured vacuum = -6.45 mmHg

OBSERVATIONS:
- Stable motor operation was observed throughout the test.
- At 80% PWM, vacuum increased smoothly and reached an approximately
  stable region near -2.9 mmHg.
- The steady-state pressure signal at 80% PWM showed only small
  fluctuations.
- At 100% PWM, vacuum magnitude progressively increased.
- A maximum measured pressure differential of -6.45 mmHg was obtained.
- No pump stall or repeated pressure collapse was observed during
  this test.

INTERPRETATION:
The direct pneumatic connection produced a cleaner and more stable
pressure response than the previous branched configuration.

The results indicate that pneumatic configuration, chamber compliance,
additional dead volume, and possible leakage paths can significantly
influence the measured vacuum response.

The test also demonstrated a relationship between PWM duty cycle and
the generated vacuum pressure differential.

LIMITATION:
The maximum measured vacuum remained substantially below the intended
vacuum range for the final breast-pump application. The present test
therefore represents preliminary low-vacuum pneumatic characterization
rather than validation of the final biomimetic breast-pump pressure
range.
